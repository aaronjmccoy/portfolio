In the Field Nov 24th 1943

Dear Parents and Grand Dad !

Today I managed to get a small kerosene lamp and can keep on writing when it is dark. I had no mail from you for a while now, but I know, that you Mother, would not let me wait long for a letter. It a difficult way of expecting answers to questions that were asked several weeks ago and had been forgotten. The main reasons for the long delays is, that the rail head for our Supplies and mail is so far away. The rail connection is also under constant attack by Partisans, the Dnjper Crossing is dangerous, and there are only a few ferries. Also the Horses are doing the main traffic on the roads, if one can call them roads at all. So far we had a very quiet front here and the new positions and Bunkers were very well constructed. Our own Bunkers even have electric lights . Last week the Division got a new Commanding General, who want to make sure he gets all the big medals. As soon as he was here, the marching started again, 50 Kilometers and more to the north where there is a Battle going on. I am at this moment in charge of the Cannon tool wagon and I am the last one to leave from here. I suppose my mail will be held with the Battery until I catch up with them. I try to adapt to all situations and mainly in the food supply and often I have to help myself to whatever is available. Since I am not at the Gun anymore but drive the supply, I am able to get a bit extra at times , today it was fried potatoes, canned peas, and dried Prunes. I get often Sugar, Flour, Onions, Salt , things like an old warrior like me needs The only disadvantage is, that in an Ammunition Supply Unit, one does not get much sleep. It is hard work to handle the shells, I wonder when this all will end? I shall not think back to former-years just keep on working and hope for a better future. Don’t let the war overtake your work, that’s what we all hope or will not happen. One day we hope to be able to get furlough but right now at this time there is none to be had.

Are you feeding the Cows well? Do you fatten pigs too for sale? Have you started the thrashing already and how is the result of the rye? I think I should not ask stupid questions like that, I need to come myself to get the answers Well, to all of you a Good Night and hope to see you soon.

Heinrich.

From: Pvt 1st Class Heinrich Bredemeier, FP # 10331 Art Regt 117

To: Family Heinrich Bredemeier, Rehren A.O. #12 near Rinteln-Weser
